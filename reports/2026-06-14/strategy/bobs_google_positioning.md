# Bob's Discount Furniture — Cross-Platform Read (Meta + Google)

*Window: Meta ads as of 2026-06-14; Google Ads Transparency Center captured 2026-06-14. Subject: Bob's. Set: Ashley, Rooms To Go, Raymour & Flanigan, Value City, American Freight, Wayfair.*

*Method note: the Google panel is a **captured sample** — the most-surfaced ~40 creatives per advertiser from the Ads Transparency Center (image/Shopping, text/search, and video formats), with text and image creatives classified the same way Meta creatives are. Treat Google **counts** as a sample, not a spend estimate; treat the Google **message mix** (what they promise, how hard they discount) as real. This is the companion to the Meta-only read — same brands, second channel.*

---

## The one-line read

On Meta, the category-wide truth was that **nobody is making anyone feel anything** — the offer table was empty and everyone coasted on evergreen brand equity. Google tells a more uncomfortable story: **the offer table is not empty on search — Bob's just isn't at it.** Wayfair and Rooms To Go run disciplined, dated, percentage-off promo against high-intent search demand; Bob's runs the same warm, evergreen brand voice it runs on Meta. That's admirable consistency and a real exposure at the same time.

---

## Where each brand actually shows up

The most important cross-platform fact is that **presence is not symmetric**. Reading Meta volume against Google presence reorders the set:

| Brand | Meta ads (in set) | Google (sampled) | Google format skew |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rooms To Go** | 679 | active | text-led (search) |
| **Wayfair** | 324 | active | balanced image + text |
| **Bob's** | 215 | active | text + image, light video |
| **Raymour & Flanigan** | 61 | active | **video-led** |
| **Ashley** | 13 | active | **video-led** (heavy) |
| **American Freight** | 7 | active | **almost all text/search** |
| **Value City** | **0** | active | text + image |

Two brands change character completely once you add the second channel:

- **Value City was effectively invisible on Meta (zero ads in the set) — and is plainly active on Google.** Its paid presence lives on search + Shopping, not paid social. Any "Value City is dormant" conclusion drawn from Meta alone is wrong; it's just fishing in a different pond.
- **American Freight looked thin on Meta (7 ads) but is running a heavy text/search program** — nearly its entire Google footprint is search ads. For a credit-tight value buyer who is actively *searching* ("furniture no credit check," "same day delivery"), that's the right channel, and AF is there.

Ashley and Raymour, quiet-ish on Meta, are **video-led on Google** (YouTube/display) — a brand-format play, not a search-intent play. Bob's and Wayfair are the two that show up substantively in *both* channels and in *both* formats.

---

## What the Google text ads actually promise

This is the part the Meta read couldn't see, and it's the most decision-useful slice. Across the captured search/text ads, classified per headline and description:

| Brand | Text ads | On-sale | Dominant offers |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wayfair** | 17 | **13** | % off, free shipping |
| **Rooms To Go** | 29 | **19** | % off, free shipping, financing |
| **Value City** | 26 | 9 | % off, **financing** |
| **American Freight** | 30 | 7 | % off |
| **Bob's** | 20 | **2** | free shipping |
| **Raymour & Flanigan** | 13 | 3 | $ off / % off (light) |

The split is stark. **Wayfair and Rooms To Go treat Google search as a promo channel** — roughly three-quarters and two-thirds of their captured text ads carry an actual stated discount, and they reach for percentage-off plus free-shipping defaults. **Bob's treats Google search the way it treats Meta** — as brand real estate. Only 2 of 20 captured Bob's text ads state a sale; the rest are brand-and-value framing: *"It's not a sale, it's Bob's,"* *"Top-rated AND affordable — Shop Bob's in-store,"* *"In-stock. Delivered fast,"* *"Bedroom sets under $600."* The loudest concrete promise Bob's makes on search is **free shipping**, not a markdown.

That is the same evergreen discipline the Meta read praised — and it carries the same cost. On Meta, evergreen is defensible because most furniture buyers aren't in-market in a given week (Ehrenberg-Bass mental-availability logic). **On search, the audience is in-market by definition** — they typed the query. Showing a high-intent searcher a brand-equity line while Wayfair shows them "20% off + free shipping" is where the consistency stops being free.

## Does the Bob's message hold across platforms?

Yes — and that's the genuinely good news. The cross-platform corpus reads as one coherent brand:

- **Meta:** "Top-rated AND affordable," $799 Consumer-Reports-recommended mattress, lifestyle-forward creative, editorial/creator partnerships.
- **Google text:** "Top-rated AND affordable — Shop Bob's in-store," "It's not a sale, it's Bob's," value-anchored price points (*"Bedroom sets under $600"*).
- **Google image/Shopping:** product-forward but tasteful — the captured Shopping creatives skew lifestyle-styled room shots and clean studio sets, not the bare white-background product grid the value tier usually defaults to.

The wedge — *quality-validated value with more taste than the price point predicts* — is intact in both channels. Bob's is not saying different things in different places, which is rarer than it sounds in this set (Rooms To Go's Meta presence is polluted by impersonator pages while its Google search program is clean and promo-disciplined — two completely different faces).

---

## Whitespace, now that there are two channels to see it in

**1. Bob's is under-promoting exactly where intent is highest.** The recommendation from the Meta read — *run one real, dated tentpole between now and July 4* — is sharper on Google. A dated "Summer Kickoff" offer surfaced on brand + category search terms would land against an audience that is actively shopping, in a channel where Bob's currently brings a brand line to a price fight. This is the lowest-risk place to test urgency because search intent converts.

**2. Free shipping is Bob's one concrete search promise — make it louder or replace it.** If free shipping is the wedge, it should be the headline, not buried. If it isn't differentiating (Wayfair defaults to it too), Bob's needs a second concrete promise on search — financing terms, in-stock/fast-delivery (already in the copy and underused), or a price-point hook like the "$600 bedroom" line that tested into the captured set.

**3. Match Value City and American Freight on intent capture, not on social.** The brands closest to Bob's price tier are competing on Google search, sometimes invisibly to a Meta-only view. A competitive read that stops at paid social will systematically under-count them.

**4. Bob's Shopping creative is a quiet strength — fund it.** The captured Google image ads are more lifestyle-styled and on-brand than the value tier norm. That's the same "more taste than the price predicts" advantage the Meta creative shows. It's working; it deserves budget and a tighter tie to the in-store/regional-growth story the Meta read flagged as under-told.
