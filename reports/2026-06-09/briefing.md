## Patterns across the set

The dominant pattern is **absence of dated, urgent promotional offers**. Across 7 furniture brands, the `offers` list is empty for every single one and only Bob's has a structured homepage promo — and even Bob's has `expiration: null` and zero urgency cues. The category is in a between-tentpoles lull (post-Memorial Day, pre-July 4 — inferred) and most brands are coasting on either evergreen "everyday low prices" copy (Bob's, American Freight), brand-equity / quality claims (Ashley "global best-seller," Raymour "80 years," Wayfair "Verified"), or carryover Memorial Day creative that should have been swapped out (Ashley `1750563672965299`, `1770583667652502`; Raymour `2641709012910177`).

The second pattern is a **format split between brick-and-mortar and pure-play**. The store-based competitors (Bob's, Ashley, Raymour, American Freight, Value City) are all using paid social to drive store visits, financing, and clearance/floor-model urgency. Wayfair is the only competitor running a true digital-native playbook — creator network at scale, app-install funnel, free-shipping threshold as the offer. Bob's is the only brick-and-mortar competitor partially mirroring the creator playbook (Apartment Therapy, House Beautiful, plus ~6 individual influencer collabs), which is a defensible mid-position. Price anchors in copy cluster at three brackets: ~$249–$399 entry mattress (Ashley), ~$499–$599 seating/dining (Bob's), ~$799 mid mattress (Bob's homepage).

## Whitespace / opportunities

- **Dated, summer-specific tentpole.** No one in the set is running a Father's Day or July 4th-anchored promo yet. Hypothesis: a 7–10 day "Father's Day / Summer Kickoff" event with a hard end-date will outperform the evergreen "everyday low prices" tone Bob's and 3 competitors are running.
- **Outdoor / patio at a real price point.** Only Wayfair (`988867120189943`, `1737595677594468`, `1337385678309989`) and Raymour (`4387227978260395`, `1477504247455240`, `1656976245857460`, `1353171700007500`) are pushing outdoor — and Wayfair's discount claim ("up to 50%") is generic. Hypothesis: a specific patio-set price-point ad (e.g., "5-piece patio set, $X") will own a bracket nobody is anchoring.
- **Speed-of-delivery as the lead message.** Raymour owns "3 days or less" / "same-day pickup" (`838528262647566`, `1466326641754605`, `2731813830538786`); Bob's mentions "ready to ship" once (`1371414068156686`) but doesn't lead with it. Hypothesis: a Bob's "in your home this week" creative will steal share from Raymour in overlapping DMAs.
- **Financing offer with a specific dollar payment.** Ashley uses "No Interest for 60 Months" abstractly; American Freight uses "$50 down" once. No one is running "$X/month for Y months on a complete room" — a concrete monthly payment for a full living room or bedroom set is whitespace.
- **TikTok-native vertical creator content.** Wayfair's creator program is Meta-skewed in the captured set; nobody has a visible TikTok shop / TikTok-Live presence in the corpus. Hypothesis: a Bob's TikTok creator series tied to the new-store openings (Pineville, Spartanburg, University City) will outperform paid Meta CPMs on local awareness.
- **Bilingual / Spanish-language creative at scale.** Bob's has exactly one Spanish ad (`1677421256790549`); no other competitor in the set has any visible Spanish creative. Hypothesis: scaling Spanish-language variants in TX/FL/Northeast Hispanic DMAs is an under-contested audience.
